Explanation

The continuous uniform distribution assigns equal probability density to every point in a bounded interval [a, b]. It is the simplest continuous distribution and serves as a baseline or reference model. Its mean is \( (a+b)/2 \) and its variance is \( (b-a)^2/12 \).

It arises naturally in: random number generation, rounding error models, arrival times uniformly distributed within a time window, and as a prior distribution in Bayesian analysis. If outcomes are not equally likely, a different distribution should be used.

Formula

\( f(x) = \dfrac{1}{b-a}, \quad a \leq x \leq b \)

\( F(x) = \dfrac{x-a}{b-a} \)

Parameters

  • a: lower bound of the interval.
  • b: upper bound of the interval (must satisfy b > a).

Worked example

Situation: A bus arrives uniformly at random between 8:00 and 8:20 (a = 0, b = 20 minutes). Let \( X \) = waiting time.

Question 1: What is \( P(X \leq 8) \)?

Solution: \[ F(8) = \frac{8 - 0}{20 - 0} = \frac{8}{20} = 0.40 \] There is a 40% chance of waiting 8 minutes or less.

Question 2: What is the median (50th percentile)?

Solution: \( (a + b)/2 = 10 \) minutes.

Question 3: What is \( P(5 \leq X \leq 12) \)?

Solution: \[ P(5 \leq X \leq 12) = \frac{12 - 5}{20} = \frac{7}{20} = 0.35 \] There is a 35% chance of waiting between 5 and 12 minutes.

How to interpret the result

The PDF is a constant (flat line) equal to \( 1/(b-a) \) over the interval [a, b] and zero outside it. Every sub-interval of the same width has the same probability, reflecting complete ignorance about where within the interval the outcome falls.

The CDF increases linearly from 0 at x = a to 1 at x = b. For any value x in [a, b], \( F(x) = (x-a)/(b-a) \) gives the fraction of the interval covered.

The quantile function is \( Q(p) = a + p(b-a) \). Use it to find the value below which a given proportion p of outcomes fall.

Frequently asked questions

  • What is the mean of a uniform distribution? The mean is (a+b)/2, the midpoint of the interval.
  • What is the variance? (b-a)²/12.
  • Can the uniform distribution be discrete? Yes, but this calculator covers the continuous case. The discrete uniform assigns equal probability to a finite set of integer values.
