What this table is for

  • One-way ANOVA: the F statistic is compared with the critical value for ν₁ = k − 1 and ν₂ = N − k degrees of freedom (k groups, N observations in total). Run the full ANOVA or compute the sample size.
  • Comparing two variances: F = s₁²/s₂², with ν₁ = n₁ − 1 and ν₂ = n₂ − 1. Run the test.
  • Left-tail values: the table only gives the value that leaves an area α to the right. For the one that leaves α to the left (needed, for example, in the two-tailed test of two variances), swap the degrees of freedom, look up that value and take its reciprocal. For example, with ν₁ = 5, ν₂ = 10 and α = 0.05: in the α = 0.05 table, column ν₁ = 10 and row ν₂ = 5, you find 4.735; the value that leaves 5% to the left is 1/4.735 = 0.211. It works because, if F follows an F(ν₁, ν₂), its reciprocal 1/F follows an F(ν₂, ν₁). For any other value, use the F calculator.

How to use these tables

F Distribution (Snedecor)

If X₁ ~ χ²(ν₁) and X₂ ~ χ²(ν₂) are independent, then F = (X₁/ν₁) / (X₂/ν₂) follows an F distribution with ν₁ and ν₂ degrees of freedom. Its density function is:

\( f(f;\nu_1,\nu_2) = \dfrac{\sqrt{\dfrac{(\nu_1 f)^{\nu_1}\,\nu_2^{\nu_2}}{(\nu_1 f+\nu_2)^{\nu_1+\nu_2}}}}{f\,B\!\left(\tfrac{\nu_1}{2},\tfrac{\nu_2}{2}\right)}, \quad f > 0 \)

The mean is ν₂/(ν₂ − 2) for ν₂ > 2. It has positive skew.

Critical values

The cell in row ν₂ and column ν₁ shows the quantile F such that P(F > f) = α. For an ANOVA at the 5% level with ν₁ = 3 groups and ν₂ = 20 residuals, look up the α = 0.05 table → F = 3.0984.

  • For equality-of-variances tests (two-sided), use α/2 in the table and reject if F > F(α/2) or F < 1/F(α/2).
  • As ν₁ → ∞ and ν₂ → ∞, the F distribution tends to 1.

Frequently asked questions

  • How do you read the Snedecor F table? Fix the significance level α; cross the numerator degrees of freedom (ν₁, in columns) with the denominator ones (ν₂, in rows). The cell is the critical value F(α, ν₁, ν₂).
  • What is the F distribution used for? To compare two variances and in ANOVA, where the F statistic is the ratio of two variances (between groups versus within groups).
  • Why is the F table not symmetric? Because the order of the degrees of freedom matters: F(α, ν₁, ν₂) is not equal to F(α, ν₂, ν₁). For the left tail the relation F(1−α, ν₁, ν₂) = 1 / F(α, ν₂, ν₁) is used.
