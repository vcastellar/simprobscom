Explanation

The beta distribution is a continuous distribution defined on the interval [0, 1], making it naturally suited to modelling proportions, probabilities, and rates. It is parameterized by two shape parameters α and β. Its mean is \( \alpha/(\alpha+\beta) \) and its variance is \( \alpha\beta/((\alpha+\beta)^2(\alpha+\beta+1)) \).

When α = β = 1 it reduces to the uniform distribution on [0, 1]. For α > 1 and β > 1 it is bell-shaped; for α < 1 or β < 1 it is U-shaped. It is the conjugate prior of the binomial in Bayesian statistics (Beta-Binomial model for A/B testing), and is used to model conversion rates, proportions in compositional data, and random effects in mixed models.

Formula

\( f(x) = \dfrac{x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}}{B(\alpha,\beta)}, \quad 0 < x < 1 \)

where \( B(\alpha,\beta) = \Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)/\Gamma(\alpha+\beta) \).

Parameters

  • α (alpha): first shape parameter (α > 0).
  • β (beta): second shape parameter (β > 0).

Worked example

Situation: An A/B test has variant A with 120 conversions out of 400 visitors (\( \hat{p}_A = 0.30 \)). Model the true conversion rate as Beta(α = 121, β = 281).

Question 1: What is \( P(p < 0.35) \)?

Solution: The CDF at 0.35 ≈ 0.9987, so there is a 99.87% credible probability the true conversion rate is below 35%.

Question 2: What is the 95% credible interval?

Solution: Quantiles at 0.025 and 0.975 give approximately [0.257, 0.346].

How to interpret the result

The PDF shows the plausibility of different values of the proportion p. A narrow, peaked distribution indicates high certainty about the value; a flat distribution indicates uncertainty. The shape of the PDF changes dramatically with α and β: both equal to 1 gives flat (uniform), both greater than 1 gives bell-shaped, both less than 1 gives U-shaped.

The CDF gives the probability that the true proportion is at most x. In a Bayesian A/B testing context, \( P(p_A < p_B) \) can be computed from the posterior beta distributions of each variant.

Credible intervals (Bayesian confidence intervals) are computed as quantiles: the interval [Q(0.025), Q(0.975)] contains the true proportion with 95% posterior probability.

Frequently asked questions

  • When is the beta distribution symmetric? When α = β.
  • What does α = β = 1 give? The uniform distribution on [0, 1].
  • How is it used in Bayesian inference? The beta is the conjugate prior for the binomial likelihood; after observing k successes in n trials, the posterior is Beta(α+k, β+n-k).
