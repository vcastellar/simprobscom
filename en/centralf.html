Explanation

The F distribution is a continuous right-skewed distribution taking only positive values, defined by two degrees of freedom: numerator df \( \nu_1 \) and denominator df \( \nu_2 \). It arises as the ratio of two independent chi-square variables each divided by their respective degrees of freedom. Its mean is \( \nu_2 / (\nu_2 - 2) \) for \( \nu_2 > 2 \). It is essential for: ANOVA (comparing means of multiple groups), tests comparing two variances (F-test), and overall significance tests in regression (the omnibus F-statistic). As both degrees of freedom grow large, the F distribution approaches normality.

Formula

\( F = \dfrac{\chi^2_1 / \nu_1}{\chi^2_2 / \nu_2} \), where \( \chi^2_1 \) and \( \chi^2_2 \) are independent chi-square variables.

Parameters

  • ν₁: numerator degrees of freedom.
  • ν₂: denominator degrees of freedom.

Worked example

Situation: A one-way ANOVA comparing 3 groups with \( n = 30 \) total observations gives an F statistic of \( F = 4.52 \) with \( \nu_1 = 2 \) and \( \nu_2 = 27 \).

Solution: The critical value at \( \alpha = 0.05 \) with 2 and 27 df is \( F^* \approx 3.35 \). Since \( 4.52 > 3.35 \), we reject \( H_0 \) that all group means are equal. The p-value is: \[ P(F > 4.52) \approx 0.020 \]

Interpretation: The p-value of 0.020 is below \( \alpha = 0.05 \), indicating statistically significant differences among at least two of the group means. Post-hoc pairwise comparisons would be needed to identify which specific groups differ.

How to interpret the result

The PDF shows the right-skewed shape of the F distribution. The shape depends heavily on both \( \nu_1 \) and \( \nu_2 \); larger degrees of freedom produce a more symmetric, concentrated distribution.

The right-tail probability \( P(F > f) = 1 - P(F \leq f) \) is the p-value in ANOVA and variance ratio tests. Most F tests are one-sided (right-tailed) since a large F statistic is evidence against the null hypothesis.

The quantile (critical value) returns \( f^* \) such that \( P(F \leq f^*) = p \). Use the 0.95 quantile for a right-tailed test at \( \alpha = 0.05 \).

Frequently asked questions

  • What does P(X ≤ x) mean? It is the cumulative probability up to value x.
  • What is the difference between left tail and right tail? The left tail accumulates up to x; the right tail measures from x towards larger values.
  • What is a percentile? It is the value of the variable that leaves a specific cumulative probability below it.
