Explanation

The negative binomial distribution models the number of failures \(X\) before achieving a fixed number of successes \(r\) in a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials, each with success probability \(p\). \(X\) takes values \(0, 1, 2, \ldots\). Mean \(= r(1-p)/p\), variance \(= r(1-p)/p^2\). When \(r=1\) it reduces to the geometric distribution.

It is also used as a generalization of the Poisson to model over-dispersed count data (variance > mean), common in ecology (species counts), epidemiology (disease cases per household), and insurance claims. As \(r\to\infty\) with \(r(1-p)/p=\mu\) fixed, it approaches a Poisson with mean \(\mu\).

Formula

\[ P(X=k) = \binom{k+r-1}{k} p^r (1-p)^k, \quad k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots \]

Parameters

  • r: number of successes (target) — a positive integer.
  • p: probability of success on each trial (\(0<p\leq 1\)).

Worked example

Situation: A researcher needs \(r=5\) successful cell cultures. Each attempt has \(p=0.6\) chance of success. Let \(X\) be the number of failed attempts before the 5th success.

Question 1: What is \(P(X=3)\)?

Solution: \[ P(X=3) = \binom{7}{3}(0.6)^5(0.4)^3 = 35 \times 0.07776 \times 0.064 \approx 0.1741 \] There is a 17.41% chance of exactly 3 failures before the 5th success.

Question 2: What is \(P(X\leq 4)\)?

Solution: Using the CDF, \(P(X\leq 4) \approx 0.5941\), so there is a 59.41% probability of completing the task within at most 4 failures. The expected number of failures is \(r(1-p)/p = 5\times 0.4/0.6 \approx 3.33\).

How to interpret the result

The PMF \(P(X=k)\) gives the probability of exactly \(k\) failures before the \(r\)th success. The CDF \(P(X\leq k)\) gives the probability of completing the task within \(k+r\) total trials. The distribution is right-skewed, especially for small \(p\). The chart shows the probability mass for each possible number of failures; the highlighted bar corresponds to the selected value.

The quantile returns the smallest integer \(k\) such that \(P(X\leq k)\) meets a given cumulative probability — useful for planning experiments and bounding the number of trials needed.

Frequently asked questions

  • What is the difference between negative binomial and binomial? Binomial fixes the number of trials and counts successes; negative binomial fixes the number of successes and counts failures (or total trials).
  • When is it better than Poisson? When count data shows overdispersion (variance > mean).
  • What is the relationship to the geometric? The geometric is the special case with \(r=1\).
