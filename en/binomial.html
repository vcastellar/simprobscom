Explanation

The binomial distribution describes how many successes occur in a fixed number \( n \) of independent yes/no trials, each with the same success probability \( p \). \( X \) takes integer values from 0 to \( n \). Its mean is \( np \) and its variance \( np(1-p) \). When \( np \geq 5 \) and \( n(1-p) \geq 5 \), the binomial approximates the normal distribution with those same parameters. For large \( n \) and very small \( p \) (so that \( np \) is moderate), it approximates a Poisson with \( \lambda = np \).

Use it when: trials are fixed in advance, independent, have only two possible outcomes, and the probability of success is the same in all. Typical examples include defective units in a quality control sample, conversions in a marketing campaign, patients responding to treatment in a clinical trial, or coin flips. If trials are not independent (sampling without replacement from a small population), use the hypergeometric distribution instead.

Formula

\( P(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k} \)

Parameters

  • n: total number of trials.
  • p: probability of success on each trial.
  • k: observed number of successes.

Worked example

Situation: A production line has \( p = 0.3 \) chance of a minor defect per part. Inspect \( n = 20 \) parts independently. Let \( X \) be the number of defective parts.

Question 1: What is \( P(X = 4) \)?

Solution: Using the PMF: \[ P(X = 4) = \binom{20}{4}(0.3)^4(0.7)^{16} \] Computing each factor: \(\binom{20}{4} = 4845\), \((0.3)^4 = 0.0081\), \((0.7)^{16} \approx 0.003323\): \[ P(X = 4) = 4845 \times 0.0081 \times 0.003323 \approx 0.1304 \] There is approximately a 13.04% chance of exactly 4 defectives.

Question 2: What is \( P(X \leq 6) \)?

Solution: Summing \( P(X=0) + P(X=1) + \cdots + P(X=6) \). The CDF of the binomial with \( n=20 \), \( p=0.3 \) at \( k=6 \) gives: \[ P(X \leq 6) \approx 0.6080 \] There is a 60.80% probability of at most 6 defectives.

Interpretation: The expected number of defects is \( np = 20 \times 0.3 = 6 \). That \( P(X \leq 6) \approx 0.608 \) confirms the mean sits near the 60th percentile, as expected for a binomial with these parameters. If the lot rejection criterion were more than 6 defects, there would be approximately a 39% chance of rejecting the lot.

How to interpret the result

The PMF \( P(X = k) \) gives the exact probability of exactly \( k \) successes in the \( n \) trials. For example, if \( P(X = 3) = 0.18 \), it means 18% of experiments are expected to produce exactly 3 successes under that model. In the chart, each vertical bar represents the probability of a specific value; the highlighted bar corresponds to the selected \( k \).

The CDF \( P(X \leq k) \) accumulates all probabilities from 0 to \( k \) inclusive. If \( P(X \leq 4) = 0.82 \), it means 82% of \( n \)-trial experiments produce 4 or fewer successes. The right tail \( P(X > k) = 1 - P(X \leq k) \) measures how unusual obtaining more than \( k \) successes would be. The green shaded area in the chart visualises the cumulative probability for the selected range; the horizontal axis shows possible counts from 0 to \( n \).

The percentile or quantile result returns the smallest integer \( k \) such that \( P(X \leq k) \geq p \). If the 90th percentile is \( k = 7 \), there is at least 90% probability of obtaining 7 or fewer successes. This is useful for defining quality acceptance thresholds, critical levels in statistical tests, or conversion targets in marketing.

Frequently asked questions

  • What does P(X ≤ x) mean? It is the cumulative probability up to value x.
  • What is the difference between left tail and right tail? The left tail accumulates up to x; the right tail measures from x towards larger values.
  • What is a percentile? It is the value of the variable that leaves a specific cumulative probability below it.
