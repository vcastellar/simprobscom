Explanation

The Poisson distribution models the number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space when events happen at a constant average rate \( \lambda \) and independently of each other. \( X \) can take any non-negative integer value. Its mean and variance are both equal to \( \lambda \). When \( \lambda \) is large (\( \lambda \geq 10 \)), the Poisson approximates a normal distribution. It arises as the limit of the binomial when \( n \) is large and \( p \) is small with \( \lambda = np \).

Use it for: number of phone calls per hour, accidents per day, mutations per DNA strand, customers arriving at a queue. If events are not truly independent or the rate varies, a negative binomial or other model may be more appropriate.

Formula

\( P(X=k) = \dfrac{e^{-\lambda}\,\lambda^k}{k!} \)

Parameters

  • λ (lambda): average number of events in the interval.
  • k: observed count of events.

Worked example

Situation: A call centre receives on average \( \lambda = 8 \) calls per hour. Let \( X \) be the number of calls in one hour.

Question 1: What is \( P(X = 5) \)?

Solution: Using the PMF: \[ P(X = 5) = \frac{e^{-8} \cdot 8^5}{5!} = \frac{0.000335 \times 32768}{120} \approx 0.0916 \] There is approximately a 9.16% chance of exactly 5 calls in one hour.

Question 2: What is \( P(X \leq 10) \)?

Solution: The CDF at \( k = 10 \) gives: \[ P(X \leq 10) \approx 0.8159 \] There is an 81.59% probability of at most 10 calls in one hour.

Interpretation: With \( \lambda = 8 \), the distribution is slightly right-skewed; most probability mass lies between 4 and 12. About 18% of hours would have more than 10 calls, which could inform staffing decisions.

How to interpret the result

The PMF \( P(X = k) \) gives the exact probability of exactly \( k \) events occurring in the interval. Each vertical bar in the chart represents the probability of a specific count; the highlighted bar corresponds to the selected \( k \).

The CDF \( P(X \leq k) \) accumulates all probabilities from 0 to \( k \) inclusive. The right tail \( P(X > k) = 1 - P(X \leq k) \) measures how unusual obtaining more than \( k \) events would be. The green shaded area in the chart visualises the cumulative probability for the selected range.

The percentile or quantile result returns the smallest integer \( k \) such that \( P(X \leq k) \geq p \). This is useful for setting service level thresholds, capacity planning, or defining critical event counts in monitoring systems.

Frequently asked questions

  • What does P(X ≤ x) mean? It is the cumulative probability up to value x.
  • What is the difference between left tail and right tail? The left tail accumulates up to x; the right tail measures from x towards larger values.
  • What is a percentile? It is the value of the variable that leaves a specific cumulative probability below it.
