Explanation

Student's t distribution is a symmetric, bell-shaped continuous distribution with heavier tails than the normal. As \( \nu \) (degrees of freedom) increases, it converges to the standard normal. It arises naturally when estimating the mean of a normally distributed population with an unknown standard deviation from a small sample. It is the basis for t-tests, confidence intervals for means, and regression coefficient inference. For \( \nu = 1 \) it becomes the Cauchy distribution; for \( \nu > 30 \) it is practically indistinguishable from the normal.

Formula

\( f(t) = \dfrac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)} \left(1 + \dfrac{t^2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}} \)

Parameters

  • ν (nu): degrees of freedom (positive integer, typically \( n-1 \) for a sample of size \( n \)).

Worked example

Situation: A sample of \( n = 16 \) observations gives \( \bar{x} = 52.3 \) and \( s = 4.1 \). Test \( H_0: \mu = 50 \) at \( \alpha = 0.05 \).

Solution: Compute the t statistic: \[ t = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s / \sqrt{n}} = \frac{52.3 - 50}{4.1 / \sqrt{16}} = \frac{2.3}{1.025} \approx 2.24 \] with \( \nu = 15 \) degrees of freedom. The two-tailed critical value at \( \alpha = 0.05 \) with 15 df is \( t^* \approx 2.131 \). Since \( |2.24| > 2.131 \), we reject \( H_0 \). The p-value is \( 2 \times P(T > 2.24) \approx 0.041 \).

Interpretation: The test statistic falls in the rejection region, providing evidence that the population mean differs from 50. With only 16 observations the t distribution with 15 df correctly accounts for the extra uncertainty in estimating the standard deviation.

How to interpret the result

The PDF shows the bell-curve shape of the t distribution. Heavier tails than the normal mean extreme values are more likely, which is why critical values are larger for small degrees of freedom and shrink towards the normal z-values as \( \nu \) grows.

The CDF \( P(T \leq t) \) gives the cumulative probability up to \( t \). The right tail \( P(T > t) = 1 - P(T \leq t) \) is used as the one-sided p-value in hypothesis tests. For a two-sided test, double the one-tailed p-value.

The quantile (critical value) returns the value \( t^* \) such that \( P(T \leq t^*) = p \). Use the 0.975 quantile for the two-tailed critical value at \( \alpha = 0.05 \), or the 0.95 quantile for a one-tailed test.

Frequently asked questions

  • What does P(X ≤ x) mean? It is the cumulative probability up to value x.
  • What is the difference between left tail and right tail? The left tail accumulates up to x; the right tail measures from x towards larger values.
  • What is a percentile? It is the value of the variable that leaves a specific cumulative probability below it.
