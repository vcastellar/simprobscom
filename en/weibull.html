Explanation

The Weibull distribution is a continuous distribution for non-negative values widely used in reliability engineering and survival analysis. It is parameterized by shape \(k\) and scale \(\lambda\). The mean is \(\lambda\,\Gamma(1+1/k)\). When \(k=1\) it reduces to the exponential distribution; when \(k=2\) it is the Rayleigh distribution. For \(k<1\) the hazard rate decreases over time (infant mortality); for \(k=1\) it is constant; for \(k>1\) it increases (wear-out failures).

The CDF is \(F(x)=1-\exp(-(x/\lambda)^k)\), which gives a straight line on a Weibull probability plot. Applications include component lifetime modelling, wind speed distributions, time to machine failure, and survival times in clinical trials.

Formula

\[ f(x) = \frac{k}{\lambda}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{k-1} \exp\!\left(-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^k\right), \quad x \geq 0 \]

Parameters

  • k (shape): controls the shape of the hazard rate (\(k>0\)).
  • λ (lambda / scale): characteristic life — the 63.2nd percentile of the distribution (\(\lambda>0\)).

Worked example

Situation: Ball bearings have a Weibull lifetime with \(k=2.5\) (shape) and \(\lambda=10{,}000\) hours (scale). Let \(X\) be the lifetime in hours.

Question 1: What is \(P(X>8{,}000)\)?

Solution: The survival function gives \[ P(X>8000) = \exp\!\left(-\left(\frac{8000}{10000}\right)^{2.5}\right) = \exp(-0.8^{2.5}) = \exp(-0.572) \approx 0.564 \] So 56.4% of bearings survive past 8,000 hours.

Question 2: What is the median lifetime?

Solution: \[ \text{Median} = \lambda(\ln 2)^{1/k} = 10000 \times (0.693)^{1/2.5} = 10000 \times 0.857 \approx 8{,}570 \text{ hours} \]

Question 3: What is the B10 life (10th percentile)?

Solution: \[ B_{10} = \lambda(-\ln 0.9)^{1/k} = 10000 \times (0.1054)^{0.4} \approx 5{,}840 \text{ hours} \] 10% of the population has failed by this time.

How to interpret the result

The PDF \(f(x)\) gives the probability density at a given lifetime \(x\). The CDF \(F(x)=P(X\leq x)\) gives the failure probability by time \(x\); the survival function \(S(x)=1-F(x)\) gives the probability of surviving beyond \(x\). The shape \(k\) tells the failure pattern: early failures (\(k<1\)), random failures (\(k=1\), exponential), or wear-out (\(k>1\)). The scale \(\lambda\) is the characteristic life — 63.2% of units fail before this time, regardless of \(k\).

The quantile (inverse CDF) returns the time \(x\) at which a given fraction of the population has failed — useful for computing B10, B50 (median) or any other B-life in reliability engineering.

Frequently asked questions

  • What does shape k mean in practice? \(k<1\): failure rate decreases (infant mortality); \(k=1\): constant failure rate (exponential); \(k>1\): failure rate increases (wear-out).
  • What is the scale λ? It is the 63.2nd percentile — 63.2% of items fail by time λ regardless of k.
  • What is B10 life? The time by which 10% of a population has failed (10th percentile), a standard reliability metric.
