Explanation

A random variable X follows a lognormal distribution if its natural logarithm ln(X) is normally distributed with mean μ and standard deviation σ. X takes only positive values and is right-skewed. Its mean is \( e^{\mu + \sigma^2/2} \) and its variance is \( (e^{\sigma^2}-1)e^{2\mu+\sigma^2} \).

It arises naturally when a quantity is the product of many independent positive factors (multiplicative growth), such as: stock prices, income distributions, city sizes, biological measurements, and repair times. Unlike the normal distribution, the lognormal cannot take negative values, making it suitable for strictly positive quantities.

Formula

\( f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\!\left(-\dfrac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \quad x > 0 \)

Parameters

  • μ (mu): mean of the natural logarithm of X.
  • σ (sigma): standard deviation of the natural logarithm of X (σ > 0).

Worked example

Situation: Household incomes in a region follow a lognormal with μ = 10.5 (log-mean) and σ = 0.8. Let \( X \) = annual income in euros.

Question 1: What is the median income?

Solution: Median \( = e^\mu = e^{10.5} \approx \)€36,315.

Question 2: What is \( P(X \leq 50{,}000) \)?

Solution: Convert to the log scale: \[ P(X \leq 50000) = P\!\left(Z \leq \frac{\ln 50000 - 10.5}{0.8}\right) = P\!\left(Z \leq \frac{10.820 - 10.5}{0.8}\right) = P(Z \leq 0.40) \approx 0.655 \] Approximately 65.5% of households earn at most €50,000.

Question 3: What is the 90th percentile?

Solution: \[ Q(0.9) = e^{\mu + 1.282\sigma} = e^{10.5 + 1.026} \approx e^{11.526} \approx \textbf{€101,400} \]

How to interpret the result

The lognormal is the natural model for right-skewed positive data. The median equals \( e^\mu \), which is always less than the mean \( e^{\mu + \sigma^2/2} \) — right skew pulls the mean upward. In practical terms, most individuals or items are below the mean, but a long right tail of large values inflates it.

Use the CDF to find percentile thresholds: \( F(x) \) gives the fraction of the population earning or measuring below x. Use the quantile function to find the income or size at a given percentile, which is useful for policy analysis, risk assessment, or SLA targets.

To work on the log scale: if \( X \sim \text{Lognormal}(\mu, \sigma) \), then \( \ln X \sim \text{Normal}(\mu, \sigma) \), so any normal distribution calculation applies after taking logarithms.

Frequently asked questions

  • What is the difference between the mean and median of a lognormal? The median is e^μ while the mean is e^(μ+σ²/2), which is always larger due to right skew.
  • How do I find the parameters from the mean and variance of X? σ² = ln(1 + Var/Mean²) and μ = ln(Mean) − σ²/2.
  • Why is the lognormal common in finance? Because percentage returns are additive in log space — multiplicative growth in prices implies lognormal price levels.
