Explanation

The hypergeometric distribution models the number of successes \(X\) in a sample of size \(n\) drawn without replacement from a finite population of \(N\) items, of which \(K\) are successes. Mean \(= nK/N\), variance \(= nK(N-K)(N-n)/(N^2(N-1))\).

It differs from the binomial in that trials are not independent: drawing one success changes the probabilities for subsequent draws. When \(N\) is very large relative to \(n\), it approximates the binomial with \(p=K/N\). Applications include quality control sampling (defective items in a batch), card games (drawing specific cards), clinical trials with limited patient pools, ecological mark-recapture studies, and acceptance sampling.

Formula

\[ P(X=k) = \frac{\binom{K}{k}\binom{N-K}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}}, \quad \max(0,\,n+K-N) \leq k \leq \min(n,K) \]

Parameters

  • N: total population size.
  • K: number of successes in the population.
  • n: sample size drawn without replacement.

Worked example

Situation: A lot of \(N=50\) items contains \(K=8\) defective ones. A quality inspector draws \(n=10\) items without replacement. Let \(X\) be the number of defective items in the sample.

Question 1: What is \(P(X=2)\)?

Solution: \[ P(X=2) = \frac{\binom{8}{2}\binom{42}{8}}{\binom{50}{10}} \approx 0.2726 \] There is a 27.26% chance of exactly 2 defectives in the sample.

Question 2: What is \(P(X\leq 1)\)?

Solution: Using the CDF, \(P(X\leq 1) \approx 0.4360\), so there is a 43.60% probability of finding at most 1 defective. The expected number of defectives is \(nK/N = 10\times 8/50 = 1.6\).

How to interpret the result

Use the hypergeometric distribution for sampling without replacement from a known finite population. The PMF is defined only for integer values in the valid range \(\max(0, n+K-N) \leq k \leq \min(n,K)\); probabilities outside this range are exactly zero. Compared to the binomial, the variance is lower by a factor of \((N-n)/(N-1)\) — the finite population correction — which approaches 1 as \(N\) grows large, recovering the binomial.

The CDF \(P(X\leq k)\) gives the probability that no more than \(k\) defectives (or successes) appear in the sample — directly useful for acceptance sampling decisions. The quantile finds the threshold \(k\) corresponding to a given cumulative probability.

Frequently asked questions

  • When should I use hypergeometric instead of binomial? When sampling without replacement from a small population (\(n/N > 0.05\)).
  • What is the finite population correction? The factor \((N-n)/(N-1)\) that reduces variance compared to binomial; it approaches 1 as \(N\) grows large.
  • Can X take any value? No — \(X\) is bounded by \(\max(0, n+K-N) \leq k \leq \min(n, K)\).
