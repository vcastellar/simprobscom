Explanation

The gamma distribution is a flexible continuous distribution for positive values, parameterized by shape α and rate β (or equivalently scale θ = 1/β). Its mean is \( \alpha/\beta \) and its variance is \( \alpha/\beta^2 \). Special cases include: exponential (α = 1), chi-square (α = ν/2, β = 1/2), and Erlang (α integer).

It models aggregated waiting times (sum of α independent exponential waiting times with rate β), insurance claim sizes, rainfall amounts, and time-to-failure in reliability engineering. The shape parameter α controls skewness: for α = 1 it is exponential, for large α it approaches the normal distribution.

Formula

\( f(x) = \dfrac{\beta^\alpha \, x^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta x}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}, \quad x > 0 \)

Parameters

  • α (alpha / shape): controls the shape of the distribution (α > 0).
  • β (beta / rate): inverse of the scale; mean = α/β.

Worked example

Situation: Machine repair times follow a Gamma with α = 3 (shape) and β = 0.5 (rate), meaning mean repair time = 3/0.5 = 6 hours. Let \( X \) = repair time.

Question 1: What is \( P(X \leq 4) \)?

Solution: The CDF gives approximately 0.3233, so there is a 32.33% chance the repair is done within 4 hours.

Question 2: What is the 90th percentile?

Solution: The quantile at 0.9 ≈ 10.65 hours. Interpretation: 90% of repairs finish within approximately 10.65 hours.

How to interpret the result

The PDF shape transitions from monotonically decreasing (α ≤ 1) to unimodal with a clear peak (α > 1). For α = 1 it is the exponential. As α grows, the distribution becomes more symmetric and bell-shaped.

The CDF gives cumulative probabilities for service levels or risk thresholds. For example, \( P(X \leq t) \) gives the probability that a repair or service completes within time t.

Quantiles are useful for setting SLA (service level agreement) targets: the p-th quantile gives the time by which a fraction p of events will have occurred.

Frequently asked questions

  • What is the relationship between gamma and exponential? The exponential distribution is a special case of the gamma with shape α = 1.
  • What is the difference between shape and rate? Shape α controls how peaked the distribution is; rate β = 1/scale controls how spread out it is.
  • How does gamma relate to chi-square? The chi-square with ν df is a gamma distribution with α = ν/2 and β = 1/2.
