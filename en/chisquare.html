Explanation

The chi-square distribution is a continuous distribution taking only non-negative values, defined by its degrees of freedom \( \nu \). It arises as the sum of \( \nu \) squared independent standard normal variables. Its mean equals \( \nu \) and its variance \( 2\nu \). It is right-skewed for small \( \nu \) and approaches normality as \( \nu \) grows. It is fundamental in: chi-square goodness-of-fit tests, tests of independence in contingency tables, confidence intervals for variance, and likelihood ratio tests. The chi-square distribution with 2 df is an exponential distribution.

Formula

\( f(x) = \dfrac{x^{\nu/2-1}\,e^{-x/2}}{2^{\nu/2}\,\Gamma(\nu/2)}, \quad x > 0 \)

Parameters

  • ν (nu): degrees of freedom.

Worked example

Situation: In a 2×2 contingency table with \( n = 200 \) observations, the chi-square test statistic is \( \chi^2 = 5.83 \) with \( \nu = 1 \) degree of freedom.

Solution: The critical value at \( \alpha = 0.05 \) with 1 df is \( \chi^{2*} = 3.841 \). Since \( 5.83 > 3.841 \), we reject the null hypothesis of independence. The p-value is: \[ P(\chi^2 > 5.83) \approx 0.016 \]

Interpretation: The observed p-value of 0.016 is below the significance level of 0.05, providing statistically significant evidence of an association between the two categorical variables. The chi-square statistic measures how far the observed cell counts deviate from the expected counts under independence.

How to interpret the result

The PDF shows the shape of the chi-square distribution. For small \( \nu \) the distribution is strongly right-skewed; as \( \nu \) increases it becomes more symmetric and bell-shaped.

The right-tail probability \( P(\chi^2 > x) = 1 - P(\chi^2 \leq x) \) is the p-value in most chi-square tests. A small p-value indicates that the observed test statistic is unlikely under the null hypothesis.

The quantile function gives the critical value \( x^* \) such that \( P(\chi^2 \leq x^*) = p \). Use the 0.95 quantile for a one-tailed test at \( \alpha = 0.05 \).

Frequently asked questions

  • What does P(X ≤ x) mean? It is the cumulative probability up to value x.
  • What is the difference between left tail and right tail? The left tail accumulates up to x; the right tail measures from x towards larger values.
  • What is a percentile? It is the value of the variable that leaves a specific cumulative probability below it.
