Explanation

The exponential distribution is a continuous distribution for non-negative values that models waiting times between events in a Poisson process. It is characterized by its rate parameter λ (or equivalently its mean 1/λ). The memoryless property is its defining feature: \( P(X > s+t \mid X > s) = P(X > t) \). Its mean is \( 1/\lambda \) and its variance is \( 1/\lambda^2 \).

Use it for: time between arrivals in a queue, component lifetime in reliability engineering, radioactive decay times, time until the next earthquake. If events can cluster (not purely random), a Weibull or gamma distribution may be more appropriate.

Formula

\( f(x) = \lambda e^{-\lambda x}, \quad x \geq 0 \)

Parameters

  • λ (lambda): rate parameter (events per unit time). Mean of distribution = 1/λ.

Worked example

Situation: Customer service calls arrive at rate \( \lambda = 0.5 \) calls/minute (mean wait = 2 min). Let \( X \) = waiting time until the next call.

Question 1: What is \( P(X \leq 3 \text{ minutes}) \)?

Solution: Using the CDF: \[ F(3) = 1 - e^{-0.5 \times 3} = 1 - e^{-1.5} \approx 1 - 0.2231 = 0.7769 \] There is a 77.69% chance the next call arrives within 3 minutes.

Question 2: Find the 90th percentile (90% of calls arrive within how many minutes).

Solution: \[ Q(0.9) = \frac{-\ln(1 - 0.9)}{0.5} = \frac{-\ln(0.1)}{0.5} \approx \frac{2.303}{0.5} \approx 4.61 \text{ minutes} \] 90% of calls arrive within approximately 4.61 minutes.

How to interpret the result

The PDF \( f(x) = \lambda e^{-\lambda x} \) gives the probability density at point x. For continuous distributions the probability at a single point is zero; area under the curve over an interval gives the probability for that interval.

The CDF \( F(x) = 1 - e^{-\lambda x} \) gives the probability that the event occurs before time x. The memoryless property means the remaining waiting time has the same distribution regardless of how long you have already waited. This makes it unique among continuous distributions.

Quantiles give service level targets: the p-th quantile is \( -\ln(1-p)/\lambda \). Use these to set deadlines (e.g. 95% of requests served within how many seconds).

Frequently asked questions

  • What does the rate λ mean? It is the average number of events per unit time. The mean of the distribution is 1/λ.
  • What is the memoryless property? The probability of waiting an additional time t does not depend on how long you have already waited.
  • How does it relate to the Poisson distribution? If events occur as a Poisson process with rate λ, the time between successive events follows an exponential distribution with that same rate.
